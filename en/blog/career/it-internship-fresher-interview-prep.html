IT Internship and Fresher Interviews: How to Prepare

Your CV gets you the interview; the interview decides whether you get the job. This guide walks through each hiring round, how to present your own project, common technical questions, the STAR method, what to do when you don't know, and what to read carefully in an offer.

Updated: October 16, 2026 11 min read – views Vinc Academy Team

Quick answer

IT fresher interviews revolve around three things: can you explain your project, do you have the fundamentals, and how do you work with others. Prepare a 2-minute project pitch, review fundamentals by topic, have 3–5 STAR stories ready, and learn to say "I don't know yet" professionally. When an offer arrives, read the salary and probation terms carefully.

Presenting your own project: the most important part

For freshers, "Tell me about a project you're proud of" comes up almost every time. It's the part you fully control, so prepare a talk of about 2 minutes using this outline:

  1. Problem: what the project solves, and for whom.
  2. Design: the main components and the data design, briefly.
  3. Your part: say "I" rather than "we" to make your contribution clear.
  4. Challenges and how you solved them: a tough bug, a design decision you had to weigh.
  5. If you did it again: what you'd improve. This shows you can assess your own work.

After your pitch, the interviewer will dig deeper. Ask yourself questions like these in advance:

  • Why this technology and not another approach?
  • How are these tables related? If records grew 100 times, what would slow down first?
  • How did you test the most important feature?
  • Which parts did AI help with, and how did you verify them?
  • What was the hardest bug, and how did you find the cause?

If you wrote your README using the outline from the previous article, most answers are already there. Reopen your code before the interview: many people forget their own project after a few months.

When you have to code live

Many freshers know the answer but freeze when someone watches them code. A 5-step routine keeps you calm and shows the interviewer how you think:

  1. Restate the problem in your own words and ask about unclear points (can the input be empty? can it contain duplicates?).
  2. Take a small example and solve it by hand first, talking through each step.
  3. State the simplest approach first, even if it isn't optimal, then suggest improvements.
  4. Write the code with meaningful names, explaining as you go.
  5. Check it yourself against your example and edge cases (empty list, single element).

Common mistake: typing in silence right after hearing the task

Situation: you hear the task and type silently for ten minutes. The code fails on an empty list, and the interviewer has no idea what you were thinking.

Cause: live coding assesses both your thinking and the result. When you stay silent, the interviewer only has the final result to judge and can't nudge you when you head the wrong way.

Prevention: follow the 5 steps above, especially restating the task and talking through your approach before typing. Practise by solving problems out loud, or ask a friend to play the interviewer.

Answering behavioural questions with the STAR method

Behavioural questions start with "Tell me about a time when…": a team disagreement, a missed deadline, learning a new technology quickly. The STAR method, recommended by many university career centres (including MIT), gives your answer a structure:

StepWhat to say
S – SituationA specific, brief situation: where and when
T – TaskYour goal or responsibility in that situation
A – ActionWhat you did, saying "I" rather than "we"
R – ResultThe outcome and what you learned; it doesn't have to be a success

Example answer to "Tell me about a time your team disagreed"

S: Software engineering course project, team of 4, two weeks before the deadline.
T: I owned the back-end; the team argued about adding features
   versus stopping to fix bugs.
A: I listed the open bugs by impact and proposed spending the first
   week fixing the 5 most serious ones, then adding one small feature
   the week after; I took on writing tests for the fixed bugs.
R: The demo ran reliably and we got a good grade. My takeaway: bringing
   concrete data into the discussion helped the team decide faster.

MIT's guidance recommends preparing 3–5 stories, each usable for several themes such as teamwork, problem solving or taking initiative. Material from course projects, internships, clubs or part-time jobs all works.

When you don't know the answer

Nobody expects a fresher to know everything. What's assessed is how you respond. A professional answer has three parts:

  1. Admit it plainly: "I'm not confident about this part."
  2. Share what you do know that's related: "My understanding is… so I'd guess…"
  3. Explain how you'd find out: "If I hit this at work, I'd read the official documentation for… and try it on a small example."

Common mistake: making up an answer to "seem to know"

Situation: asked about a concept you've never studied, you answer confidently with a guess. The interviewer asks one more question, and your answers start to contradict each other.

Cause: interviewers usually ask follow-ups to test depth, so a made-up answer rarely survives two questions. At that point what they note is no longer a knowledge gap but a lack of honesty, a much heavier minus for a role that requires teamwork.

Prevention: use the 3-part answer above. "I don't know yet, but I'd find out by…" is usually rated higher than a confident wrong answer.

Questions worth asking the interviewer

Most interviews end with "Do you have any questions?". It's your chance to assess the company and show you're serious. For freshers, the most useful questions are:

  • How are newcomers guided in the first month? Is there a mentor?
  • Does the team have a code-review process?
  • What technologies does the project or product I'd join use?
  • What are the criteria for passing probation?

Reading an offer and the probation period

When you receive an offer for regular employment in Vietnam (not an internship programme under a separate agreement with your university or the company), probation is governed by the 2019 Labour Code. Key points for freshers:

ItemRuleBasis
Maximum probationAt most 60 days for jobs requiring college-level or higher professional or technical qualifications (such as engineers and specialists)Article 25
Number of probationsEach job can be probated only onceArticle 25
Probation payAgreed by both parties, at least 85% of the salary for that jobArticle 26
End of probationThe employer must notify the result; if passed, the contract continues or a labour contract is signedArticle 27
Cancelling during probationEither party may cancel without notice and without compensationArticle 27

Beyond these points, ask clearly about the probation criteria, the official salary after probation and when raises are reviewed. To judge whether the salary is reasonable, compare it with the median salary table in the IT career roadmap 2026 article. This is for reference only; for a specific situation, read the original legal text or ask someone with legal expertise.

Summary

  • Know the hiring rounds in advance so you prepare what each one assesses.
  • A 2-minute project pitch (problem → design → your part → challenges → if you did it again) is a fresher's strongest asset.
  • When coding live: restate the task, solve an example by hand, talk through your approach, then type and self-check.
  • Prepare 3–5 STAR stories; when you don't know, admit it, share what's related and how you'd find out, never make things up.
  • Read the offer carefully: in Vietnam, probation is at most 60 days for jobs requiring college-level qualifications, with pay of at least 85%.

Frequently asked questions

What do IT fresher interviews usually ask? ▾
Usually three parts: presenting and digging into your project, fundamentals (programming, data structures, databases, web, Git) and behavioural questions about teamwork and handling difficulties. Some companies add a live coding task or a language test.
What should I say when I don't know the answer? ▾
Say honestly that you're not sure, then share the related knowledge you do have and how you would research or reason your way to the answer. Don't make things up: interviewers usually follow up, and a confident wrong answer leaves a worse impression than admitting you don't know.
How long can probation be for an IT fresher in Vietnam? ▾
Under Article 25 of Vietnam's 2019 Labour Code, jobs requiring college-level or higher professional or technical qualifications can have a probation period of at most 60 days, and each job can be probated only once. Software engineer roles usually fall into this group.
What is the minimum probation salary? ▾
Under Article 26 of the 2019 Labour Code, probation pay is agreed by both parties but must be at least 85% of the salary for that job. The parties can agree on more, including 100%.
Should a fresher negotiate salary? ▾
You can, if you have grounds: check the role's median salary in public salary reports and point to what you bring, such as relevant projects or skills the company needs. For freshers, learning opportunities, a mentor and the raise path after probation are worth weighing as much as the starting salary.
